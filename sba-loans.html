What is an SBA loan?

An SBA loan is made by a bank or other approved lender, with part of the loan guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration. That guarantee lets lenders offer longer terms than they otherwise might.

American Commerce Capital is not the SBA and is not affiliated with, endorsed by or backed by it. This page explains how these loans work so you can decide whether to pursue one or a faster option.

Benefits of an SBA loan

Longer terms

Payments are spread over more time.

Many uses

Working capital, equipment, real estate and more, depending on the program.

Larger amounts

Programs exist for a wide range of needs.

Built for small business

Designed for companies that may not fit standard bank lending.

How does an SBA loan work?

You apply through an SBA-approved lender, which reviews your business and submits the request under an SBA program. The process asks for detailed documents and takes time.

If you need capital sooner, many owners use faster financing to cover the gap. A Capital Specialist can walk you through what ACC offers and what an SBA loan would involve.

What to weigh.

SBA loans are known for longer terms. The trade-off is time and paperwork: more documents, more review and a longer wait.

If timing is the constraint, compare the cost of waiting with the cost of faster capital. We will give you a straight answer on which fits.

SBA loan or faster financing?

Both put capital to work. Where they differ is how you receive it and how you pay it back.

Is it the right choice?

An honest look at both sides. If another product fits you better, we will tell you.

It makes sense when

  • You can wait for funding
  • You have strong records and documents
  • The need is planned and long-term
  • You want the longest terms
  • Your business has an established history

It is the wrong choice when

  • You need capital this week
  • Your records are incomplete
  • The business is very new
  • The amount you need is small and short-term
  • An opportunity will pass before approval

Frequently Asked Questions

Does ACC make SBA loans?
No. SBA loans are made by SBA-approved lenders. ACC is not affiliated with the SBA. ACC provides its own business financing directly.
How long does an SBA loan take?
It varies by lender and program, and is generally measured in weeks or more.
What do I need to apply?
Lenders typically ask for financial statements, tax returns and a description of how the funds will be used.
Where do I apply?
Through an SBA-approved lender. The SBA lists them at sba.gov.
What if I cannot wait?
Talk to a Capital Specialist about faster options. Decisions come in as little as 4 hours and funding can arrive as soon as the same day. Timing depends on your file.
Can I use both?
Some owners use faster capital now and pursue an SBA loan for long-term needs. Whether that works depends on your situation and each lender's terms.
