What is a working capital loan?

A working capital loan is a lump sum your business receives upfront and pays back over a short, fixed schedule. It is built for needs that are close at hand: a large order, a slow month, a repair that cannot wait.

Because the term is short, the review focuses on how your business is performing right now. Owners use it when timing matters more than stretching payments over years.

Benefits of a working capital loan

Capital in days

You know each payment and the end date before you sign.

Terms are typically 3 to 15 months.

Use the funds wherever the business needs them.

How does a working capital loan work?

Apply with a short form and recent business bank statements. A Capital Specialist reviews your file and, if approved, walks you through the amount, the total cost and the payment schedule.

Once you sign, funds are sent to your business bank account. Payments are collected automatically on the agreed schedule until the total is paid.

What short-term capital costs.

Short-term financing is priced for speed and access, so it generally costs more than a long bank loan. The right question is whether the capital will earn or save more than it costs.

ACC shows you the total payback in dollars and every payment before you accept. If a slower, cheaper option fits you better, we will tell you.

Is it the right choice?

An honest look at both sides. If another product fits you better, we will tell you.

It makes sense when

  • You have a clear, near-term use for the money
  • You want a predictable payment
  • The return comes within months
  • A bank is too slow for the timing
  • Your revenue is consistent

It is the wrong choice when

  • You need years to pay it back
  • You are covering ongoing losses
  • Your revenue swings sharply
  • You can wait for lower-cost bank financing
  • You have no specific plan for the funds

Terms to understand

The words you will see in an offer, in plain English.

Principal

The amount sent to your business.

Term

How long you have to pay it back.

Total payback

Everything you pay over the full term.

Payment schedule

How often payments are collected.

Origination fee

A fee some financing includes at funding. Yours is listed in your agreement.

Early payoff

Paying the remaining balance ahead of schedule. Your agreement explains how it works.
