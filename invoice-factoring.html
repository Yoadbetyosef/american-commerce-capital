What is invoice factoring?

Invoice factoring turns money your customers owe you into cash you can use today. You receive most of an invoice upfront, and the rest, less a fee, when your customer pays.

It is built for businesses that sell to other businesses on payment terms. The strength of your customers matters as much as your own.

Benefits of invoice factoring

Stop waiting on customers

Cash arrives when you invoice, not when they pay.

Grows with your sales

More invoices means more available capital.

Based on your receivables

The review looks at who owes you money.

No fixed monthly payment

It settles when your customer pays the invoice.

How does invoice factoring work?

You send the invoices you want to fund. After they are verified, you receive an advance on their value in your business bank account.

When your customer pays the invoice, the remaining amount is released to you, less the agreed fee. You choose which invoices to fund.

What factoring costs.

The cost of factoring is a fee on the invoice, and it generally depends on how long your customer takes to pay. Faster-paying customers cost less to fund.

ACC shows you the advance, the fee and what you receive at the end before you fund an invoice.

Invoice factoring or line of credit?

Both put capital to work. Where they differ is how you receive it and how you pay it back.

Is it the right choice?

An honest look at both sides. If another product fits you better, we will tell you.

It makes sense when

  • You invoice other businesses
  • Customers pay in 30 to 90 days
  • Your customers pay reliably
  • Growth is tying up your cash
  • You want funding that scales with sales

It is the wrong choice when

  • You sell to consumers
  • Customers pay at the time of sale
  • Invoices are in dispute
  • Most receivables are far past due
  • Your margins are too thin for the fee

Terms to understand

The words you will see in an offer, in plain English.

Advance rate

The share of an invoice you receive upfront.

Reserve

The remainder, released when your customer pays.

Factoring fee

The cost of funding the invoice.

Recourse

Whether you are responsible if a customer does not pay.

Verification

Confirming the invoice with your customer.

Notification

Telling your customer where to send payment.

Best ways to use it

  • Payroll while waiting on customers
  • Materials for the next job
  • Taking on larger contracts
  • Fuel and freight costs
  • Supplier payments
  • Smoothing seasonal swings

Frequently Asked Questions

Is factoring a loan?
No. You are selling invoices, not borrowing against them.
Will my customers know?
In many arrangements customers are told where to send payment. Your Capital Specialist explains how it would work for you.
What if a customer does not pay?
It depends on the terms. Your agreement states who carries that risk before you fund anything.
How fast is funding?
Decisions come in as little as 4 hours and funding can arrive as soon as the same day. Timing depends on your file.
Do I have to factor every invoice?
You and your Capital Specialist agree on which invoices to fund.
How much can I get?
ACC funds from $25,000 to $1,000,000.
