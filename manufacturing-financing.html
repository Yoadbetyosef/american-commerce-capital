Financing for manufacturers

Manufacturing ties up cash at every step. Materials are bought, labor is paid and goods are shipped, and then the invoice waits.

ACC helps cover the stretch between buying materials and being paid for the finished order.

Why manufacturers need working capital

Raw materials upfront

Suppliers want payment before your customer pays you.

Machinery

Equipment is expensive to buy, maintain and replace.

Large orders

A big order can strain cash before it adds to it.

How cash flow works in this business

A typical order means paying for materials at the start, labor through production and freight at the end. Customers then pay on terms of 30 to 90 days.

Growth makes this harder, not easier, because each new order adds to the cash tied up before payment arrives.

What manufacturers finance

Raw materials

Inputs for confirmed orders.

Machinery

New equipment or major repairs.

Payroll

Production staff and overtime.

Tooling

Dies, molds and fixtures.

Facility costs

Space, power and upgrades.

Freight

Getting goods to the customer.

When financing makes sense

A good fit, and a poor one.

A good fit

  • Consistent revenue in the business account
  • A clear gap between costs and payment
  • A specific use for the funds
  • Work or orders already lined up
  • The capital will earn more than it costs

A poor fit

  • Covering losses instead of timing
  • Revenue that is falling
  • No plan for how the funds will be used
  • Several existing advances already in place
  • A need that can wait for lower-cost financing
